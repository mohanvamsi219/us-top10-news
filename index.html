Top 10 TodayOct. 10, 2026
  1. 1
    Isaias Weakens After Florida Landfall, Knocks Out Power ↗
    BBC News

    Hurricane Isaias, the first of the 2026 Atlantic season, made landfall on Florida's northern Gulf Coast and was downgraded to a post-tropical cyclone. Saturday morning more than 400,000 customers in Florida and 300,000 in Alabama lacked power.

  2. 2
    Trump Strikes Deal With Putin for Russian Diesel ↗
    AP / MPR News

    Trump said Russia will supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel at once, with more to follow, as U.S. diesel averages $6.28 a gallon. Treasury eased diesel sanctions; Zelenskyy called it "a weak decision," and Senate Democrats called it a betrayal.

  3. 3
    US Sanctions the International Criminal Court Itself ↗
    CBS News

    Secretary of State Rubio announced sanctions on the ICC itself, blocking its U.S.-linked assets and barring most U.S. business with the court, with a 180-day window for some operations. The ICC called it "an assault on the rule of law."

  4. 4
    Two Philadelphia Officers Critically Hurt in Shotgun Ambush ↗
    Philadelphia Inquirer

    Two officers answering a man-with-a-gun call in West Philadelphia early Saturday were met with shotgun fire; one was shot three times and the other 12 times. Police found the suspect in a tree and took him into custody.

  5. 5
    Judge Blocks Deportation of Man ICE Shot ↗
    ABC7 New York

    A federal judge on Friday barred the government from deporting Oscar Belgal, shot by an ICE agent in Manhattan with his 5-year-old son in the car. DHS says agents feared for their lives; Gov. Hochul called for a pause in ICE actions.

  6. 6
    Trump Creates Committee to Probe Fed's Lisa Cook ↗
    CNBC

    Trump formed a three-person committee to recommend whether he has cause to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook over mortgage fraud allegations she denies. Cook is expected at a White House hearing Nov. 5; her lawyers doubt it is legitimate.

  7. 7
    Both Parties Blast Plan to Livestream Military Execution ↗
    Roll Call

    Lawmakers in both parties criticized Defense Secretary Hegseth's plan to livestream the Dec. 3 firing-squad execution of Fort Hood shooter Nidal Hasan. VP Vance said he doubts it will happen and won't watch if it does.

  8. 8
    Navi Pillay Wins 2026 Nobel Peace Prize ↗
    Reuters / CBC News

    South Africa-born jurist Navi Pillay, 85, a former U.N. human rights chief and now an International Court of Justice judge, won the Nobel Peace Prize. The committee called her one of the great defenders of international legal principles.

  9. 9
    Trump Taps Katie Zacharia as Press Secretary ↗
    CNBC

    The White House tapped Katie Zacharia, a lawyer and Trump Media communications adviser, as its next press secretary, succeeding Karoline Leavitt, who left in late August. She briefly served as a DHS spokeswoman earlier this year.

  10. 10
    Stocks Rise to Close Out Record-Setting Week ↗
    AP / WTOP

    The S&P 500 rose 0.6% Friday to 7,811.54, near its all-time high, while the Dow gained 0.8% to 51,654.95 and the Nasdaq added 0.6%. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.24%.

Immigration BriefingOct. 10, 2026
  1. 1
    Judge Blocks Deportation of Man ICE Shot ↗
    ABC7 New York

    A federal judge on Friday barred the government from deporting Oscar Belgal, 28, shot by an ICE agent in Marble Hill with his 5-year-old son in the car. DHS Secretary Mullin said agents feared for their lives; Gov. Hochul called for a pause in ICE actions in New York.

  2. 2
    Labor Dept Subpoenas Nine Universities Over J-1 Visas ↗
    Inside Higher Ed

    VP JD Vance said the Labor Department is investigating Harvard, Yale, Stanford, MIT, Brown, Caltech, Arizona State, UC Davis and Pitt over alleged J-1 visa abuse, and subpoenas have been served. Several schools said they comply with the law and will respond.

  3. 3
    H-1B Workers Weigh Fallout of PERM Green Card Freeze ↗
    Business Standard

    After DOL suspended PERM green card processing for TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Cognizant and three others, lawyers say H-1B petitions were not banned, but extensions and transfers at those firms may face more scrutiny.

  4. 4
    Judge Restores Biometrics for Detained Immigrants ↗
    Financial Express

    Judge Amir Ali blocked a USCIS policy that stopped collecting fingerprints and photos from detained immigrants with pending applications, restoring the earlier process nationwide. It does not grant status or release anyone.

  5. 5
    Court Rules Remain in Mexico Policy Unlawful ↗
    The Independent

    A federal judge found the Remain in Mexico policy illegally blocked asylum seekers' access to protection, counsel and fair hearings; the ruling was unsealed Friday. Advocates say only 7 percent of people subjected to it found lawyers.

  6. 6
    Immigration Board Limits Asylum From 40 Democracies ↗
    DOJ / Board of Immigration Appeals

    In Matter of B-S-A-N-, issued Oct. 9, the Board of Immigration Appeals told immigration judges to generally deny asylum claims from nationals of 40 developed democracies, including EU states, Canada and the U.K., absent extraordinary circumstances.

  7. 7
    Judge Says ICE Agents Falsified Records in Bronx ↗
    Gothamist

    Judge Gary Brown, a Trump appointee, ruled ICE agents illegally arrested two men in the Bronx in May, including a U.S. citizen, then gave false testimony and falsified records. ICE has 21 days to say how it will keep future arrests lawful.

  8. 8
    Medicaid Cuts Drop Refugees and Legal Immigrants ↗
    AP / ABC News

    Changes effective Oct. 1 cut Medicaid for certain legal immigrants, including refugees, asylum seekers and trafficking victims; green card holders were not affected. KFF estimates over 281,000 immigrants in nine states and D.C. lose coverage.

  9. 9
    ICE Finalizes Civil Fines Rule After Court Block ↗
    Hindustan Times

    ICE finalized a rule on civil immigration penalties, Bloomberg Law reported Oct. 9, days after a Massachusetts judge stayed the earlier interim rule. The final rule keeps the framework but allows some late appeals when mail delays prevent timely responses.

  10. 10
    DHS Silent on ICE Pause During Hurricane Isaias ↗
    AP

    DHS would not tell AP on Friday whether non-criminal immigration enforcement will be suspended in states hit by Hurricane Isaias. Past administrations, including before Hurricane Irma in 2017, said such operations would pause in affected areas.
